[Scene sadly prologue]
Mai is a normal high school girl carrying sullen teenage resentment. She feels suffocated, lonely, and constantly misunderstood by her family. One evening, an explosive argument erupts in the living room over hidden bad grades, school rumors, and her cold silence. Heated words cut deep: her father slams the table in frustration while her mother looks on, exhausted to tears. In blind, wounded fury, Mai screams that she hates them and **wishes everyone would just disappear**. She storms up to the second floor, slams her bedroom door shut, buries her face in her pillow, and weeps until exhaustion pulls her to sleep.

[Scene morning?]
Mai wakes up to an unnatural, suffocating quiet. The morning air feels unusually damp and icy. Outside her window, the familiar neighborhood is completely swallowed by a thick, motionless wall of **milk-white fog**. When she tries her bedroom door, the brass knob rattles uselessly—the latch has **jammed tight from the inside**. Feeling an immediate prick of claustrophobia, she searches her room for a way out. Through an adjoining doorway into the private bathroom, water drips rhythmically from a **faucet running on its own**. Inside the medicine cabinet behind a fogged mirror, she finds the **spare bedroom key**. But when she glances at the glass, her **reflection's eyes linger a second too long**, staring back with hollow sorrow before matching her movement.

[Scene empty breadfast]
Unlocking the door, Mai tiptoes down the creaking staircase into the downstairs hallway. Still clinging to her stubborn teenage pride, she calls out in an irritated voice that she wants breakfast, hoping to mask her growing unease. **No one replies**. In the kitchen, the dining table is cold and bare. A skillet rests on the unlit stove holding **cold fried chicken and half-burnt toast** from last night. An unwashed coffee cup sits beside an open refrigerator humming in the void. There is no sound of cooking, no morning news, and **no footsteps anywhere**.

[Scene living room ground zero]
Mai steps into the living room, frozen as she looks upon the exact scene of last night's fight. An overturned ceramic mug lies **shattered across the hardwood floor**, a dark coffee stain dried into the grain like an open scar. The television screen is on, glowing with **muted grey static** that emits a low, ceaseless hum. On the coffee table beside the shattered mug sits an open ledger: her **father's overtime shifts ledger** and tight household budgets, sitting beside **unpaid tuition slips** for Mai's cram school that she had repeatedly skipped. The weight of her parents' private burdens starts creeping into the silence.

[Scene front door and the boundary]
Growing desperate, Mai heads to the front door to check on the street. She turns the deadbolt and steps out onto the front porch. The neighborhood is completely severed; beyond the small front garden gate, the road fades into an **opaque white void**. As she tries to step past the threshold of the gate, a **crushing heaviness grips her chest** and fog fills her throat, forcing her to cough and retreat back toward the house. The world has isolated her. **She cannot run away** from what has occurred.

[Scene the silent garden]
Mai rounds the side of the house into the overgrown backyard garden. Weeds have strangled the flowerbeds her mother used to tend patiently every weekend. Tucked in the corner under a dying persimmon tree sits an **empty dog house with a rusted chain** draped over weathered wood. Beside it rests a **faded food bowl with scratched initials of a childhood pet** that passed away years ago—a quiet loss the family never openly talked about, marking the beginning of their emotional distance. Among the dry leaves near the kennel, Mai uncovers her **mother's rusted pruning shears** and a locked **biscuit tin containing old family photographs**.

[Scene the first rest]
Holding the keepsake tin, a sudden wave of dizzying fatigue crashes over Mai. Touching the relics of her family's past triggers a **cold, throbbing pain behind her temples**. The silence in the house becomes deafening. Clinging to the desperate hope that this entire morning is merely a vivid nightmare, Mai retreats to her bed, pulls her blankets tight, and **closes her eyes to try to sleep**.

[Scene reality shift: the rotting guilt]
When Mai opens her eyes, the room has decayed. The soft pastel wallpaper peels away in **weeping, black rot**. Furniture is warped, smelling faintly of old ashes and dried tears. In the corners of the room, **shadowy humanoid silhouettes** tremble in silent grief. The quiet fog has been replaced by the sound of her **parents' muffled weeping** echoing through the floorboards. To survive and dispel the black vines choking the hallway, Mai must interact with the manifestations of her guilt, **piece together the shattered ceramic fragments of her father's mug**, and confront the truth of how deeply her words wounded them.

[Scene the market in the mist]
Having mended the emotional tear inside the house, the suffocating fog at the front gate recedes, revealing a narrow pedestrian alley leading toward the nearby market. Mai ventures outside into the eerie silence. The normally bustling shopping street is an abandoned wasteland of tipped-over plastic stools, rusted roll-down metal gates, and fruit crates filled with decaying produce rotting beneath flickering amber streetlamps. Near a closed butcher stall, Mai discovers her **mother's dropped grocery basket**, containing a receipt showing **her mother had bought Mai's favorite treats while skipping lunch for herself**.

[Scene the market scale trial]
A dense wall of shifting black thorns blocks the exit from the market arcade. Exhausted from the oppressive atmosphere, Mai finds shelter in an abandoned fruit stand cot, pulling a damp tarp over herself and **closing her eyes to sleep**. She shifts into the rotting reality. The market becomes a surreal iron slaughterhouse of hanging hooks and **towering brass balance scales**. To proceed, Mai must solve an environmental puzzle **weighing her parents' hidden sacrifices against her own perceived unfairness**, realizing how much humiliation her parents absorbed from lenders and employers just to preserve her comfort.

[Scene the school gate]
The path clears further, opening into the foggy courtyard of her high school. The massive campus is shrouded in mist, with only the entrance courtyard, a silent central corridor, and her homeroom on the second floor remaining intact. Lockers stand half-open, personal shoes and notebooks scattered as if vanished mid-stride. Outside the faculty office, Mai finds a crumpled **guidance counseling note detailing bullying rumors** targeting Mai—the real reason her grades had dropped, which she had proudly kept hidden from her parents out of fear of looking weak.

[Scene classroom 2-B]
Mai enters classroom 2-B. The desks have been pushed into an ominous ring facing her lone desk in the center. The chalkboard behind is covered in scratched, frantic chalk writing repeating her own spoken words: *"I wish everyone would disappear."* Sitting down at her desk, the emotional resonance becomes unbearable. Mai rests her head on her arms and **closes her eyes**. In the rotting shift, shadowy faceless classmates encircle her with whispering taunts, while the **protective shadow of her mother** stands between Mai and the shadows, absorbing their harsh barbs until she collapses.

[Scene the lonely park]
Exiting the school with tears streaming down her face, Mai follows the faint, rhythmic screech of rusted iron cutting through the quiet fog. She arrives at the neighborhood park. The playground sits abandoned beneath the overcast sky, except for a **single rusted swing swaying gently** on its own. This is the park where her father taught her to ride a bicycle and pushed her on the swings when she was a child, promising that no matter what happened in the world, home would always be her sanctuary. Beneath the woodchips under the swing, Mai unearths a forgotten **metal time capsule**: a **childhood drawing of the family smiling under the sun**, preserved by her father inside his work briefcase.

[Scene the collapse of the wish]
Clutching the drawing against her chest, the fog begins to convulse violently. The soft white mist and the black rotting rot collide into a tempest. The town diorama begins to tear apart at the seams. Mai realizes with piercing clarity that **the fog was never an external curse—it was the literal realization of her own selfish wish**. Her parents didn't abandon her; she had banished them into the void to escape confronting her own vulnerability. The only way to reverse the wish is to return to where the curse was born: **the living room ground zero**.

[Scene the living room reunion]
Mai sprints back through the dissolving streets, bursting through the front door into her house. In the living room, the **shadows of her mother and father sit motionless at the dining table**, encrusted in crystalline ash and weeping black rot. Mai does not flee or look away. She approaches the table, placing down every recovered memory: the **restored ceramic mug**, the **budget ledger**, the **counseling note**, and the **childhood drawing**. Falling to her knees between them, Mai embraces their petrified silhouettes, sobbing as she admits her selfishness and pleads with them to come back.

[Scene dawn over the fog]
The black rot recedes, dissolving into gentle golden dust. The oppressive white fog outside the windows thins and scatters under the warmth of the rising morning sun. The heavy cold in the room softens into life. Mai opens her swollen eyes as the gentle clatter of porcelain and the sizzle of breakfast on the stove reaches her ears. From the kitchen doorway, **her mother's caring voice softly calls her name**, asking if she is ready to eat. Mai stands up, wiped of her stubborn anger, and walks toward the light.
