### 1. The Domestic Core (The House & Garden)

Keeping four of the seven spaces inside and around the house lets your team share 3D materials, lighting setups, and architectural props, drastically cutting modeling hours:

- **Her Room:** The hub. It starts familiar and becomes claustrophobic. It’s where her diary, teenage hobbies, and private guilt live.
    
- **The Bathroom:** The most vulnerable domestic space. Mirrors allow for subtle psychological horror—reflections lagging behind, water running on its own, or confronting her own hollow eyes in the glass.
    
- **The Living Room:** The crime scene of the argument. An overturned mug, shattered glass on the floor, an unfinished meal, or a silenced television buzzing with low static. It is ground zero for her guilt.
    
- **The Garden with the Empty Dog House:** This is a devastating environmental detail. An empty chain or worn-out food bowl immediately signals past loss, neglect, or broken promises without needing a single cutscene or voice line. It grounds her family’s broken home in real history.
    

### 2. The Routine Spaces (The Foggy Exterior)

Instead of building a continuous open world, dense fog lets you treat the outdoor zones as isolated "islands" connected by short, linear street paths:

- **The Nearby Market:** Cold, abandoned metal stalls, plastic stools tipped over, and rotting produce under dim streetlamps. It contrasts her domestic guilt with the sudden, eerie silence of the community she saw every morning.
    
- **Her School:** Confining this to just a foggy courtyard, a quiet hallway, and her specific classroom avoids the trap of modeling an entire campus. It holds the origin of the rumors or bad grades that caused her parents' anger.
    
- **The Lonely Park:** A single rusted swing moving gently in the fog. It serves as a visual metaphor for childhood innocence—the time before expectations, parental fights, and teenage resentment took over.
